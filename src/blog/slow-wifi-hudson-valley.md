# Why Is My Wi-Fi Slow? Common Causes in Hudson Valley Homes

"Everything works, it's just... slow." If that sounds like your home, you're not alone. In Hudson Valley homes, slow Wi-Fi usually comes down to a handful of repeatable causes — and most of them are fixable without running cable through every wall.

### The usual suspects

- **Plaster and masonry.** Many Rivertown and Hudson Valley homes have plaster-and-lath walls, which absorb Wi-Fi far more than drywall. If your router is in a plaster-walled room, the signal may barely reach the next room.
- **The router is in the wrong place.** The most common fix we make is moving a router from a closet or corner office to a more central location — sometimes literally all that's needed.
- **One router trying to cover everything.** A single access point rarely covers a rambling Victorian or a multi-level Colonial. Mesh systems or multiple access points are usually the answer.
- **Old wiring and interference.** 100-year-old electrical wiring and neighbors on overlapping channels can degrade performance. A site survey can pinpoint the problem.

### How we diagnose it

We start with a network assessment — walking your home with a signal meter, checking where coverage drops, and identifying interference. Then we propose options:

- Repositioning your current router (free, and often enough).
- A mesh Wi-Fi system tuned to your home's layout.
- Hardwired backhaul or access points where mesh isn't enough.

### The bottom line

Slow Wi-Fi in an older home is rarely "your internet provider's fault." It's almost always a coverage problem — and coverage problems have solutions. A [smart home audit](/pricing) includes a look at your network, so you can find out exactly what's wrong before you spend money.
